The next morning, Saturday 9 March, Hollis showed me the logbook. The entries were her father's. Amos Wren had kept the hut for thirty-eight years.

"He wrote down the weather, every party that came through, and every rescue," she said. "This is the avalanche of 1999. I was forty-one."

I turned the pages. Amos wrote in a small, careful hand, and the black ink had gone brown. At the foot of one page he had drawn a sledge with a blue runner and a name printed beside it: _Kestrel_.

Hollis closed the logbook and put it back on the shelf. The hut smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool. Outside, the sky was as grey as the stones, and a raven landed on the rail.
