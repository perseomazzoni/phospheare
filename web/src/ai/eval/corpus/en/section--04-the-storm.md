The storm came on Monday 11 March, early in the afternoon. Hollis went out to check the ropes and I went with her, holding the lantern. The wind shook the shutters and the snow came sideways.

"My father said this hut has stood for a hundred and twenty years," Hollis shouted over the noise. "It will stand tonight."

We stayed out for three hours. Hollis checked the anchors, cleared the vents, and wrote everything down in a new notebook with a blue cover. I held the lantern and tried not to look at the edge.

Around nine the wind dropped. Hollis took off her black hat and ran a hand through her white hair. "Now you write," she said, and gave me the notebook.
