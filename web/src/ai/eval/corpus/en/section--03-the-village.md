I walked down to the village. Harrow End had perhaps three hundred people, and all of them seemed to know who I was. The baker, a tall man called Tobias, gave me a warm loaf and would not take my money.

"You're the one writing about the hut," Tobias said. "Has Hollis told you about the _Kestrel_?"

I said she had. He wiped his hands on his white apron and looked up at the pass. "That night Amos carried the boy four miles through the storm. He was sixty years old."

I climbed back up with the loaf under my arm. It took two hours, as Hollis had said. At the hut she was painting the door red.
