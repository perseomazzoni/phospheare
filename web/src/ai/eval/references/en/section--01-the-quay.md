Ilse Varga was twenty-six that autumn of 1911, and she had worked her father's ferry since she was a girl. Tom limped along the quay behind the magistrate, his cap in his hand, while she coiled the rope.

'God sent the water,' said the widow Hollis at the ferry steps, as she said to every passenger, 'and He will send it again.' Ilse helped her aboard and did not argue.

Nobody on the quay spoke of Ilse's mother.
