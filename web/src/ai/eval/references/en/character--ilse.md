## Personal history

- **Formative experiences:**
  <!-- Hint: Consider the upbringing, experiences and relationships that shaped the character. Identify those that still influence their life. -->
  - Ilse grew up on the water and in the ferry house, among ropes, tide tables and the men who came to drink with her father after the last crossing. She learned the strait before she learned to read: where the current bends under the lighthouse, which buoys drift in a north wind, how long the channel stays open after the bell. Her father taught her the knots and the silence that went with them, and her mother taught her nothing she can remember except a song about a drowned sailor, which she still hums when she is frightened. Her mother may have drowned in the crossing of 1899, when Ilse was fourteen; the body was never found, the family never agreed on what happened, and Ilse still half expects a letter. After that year she stopped going to school and took the morning crossings herself, because her father could not face the water at dawn. The men on the quay laughed at her for a season and then stopped laughing, and she has never forgiven them the season. She keeps her mother's comb in the drawer of the ferry house, and she has told no one that she keeps it.
