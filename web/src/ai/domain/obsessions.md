# The obsessions

The things a professional cannot stop noticing, one entry each. A persona
holds a handful of them at a time — a preset (`personas/*.md`) — and the
prompt carries only those. Each entry:

- `notices` — one line, what the persona sees; what the prompt carries.
- `asks` — the same thing as a question, for the rooms that ask rather than
  report (drafting, alpha).
- `signals` — what it looks like on the page.
- `exceptions` — when it is a choice, not a fault.
- `severity` — `craft` (taste, raised once), `clarity` (the reader loses the
  thread), `contract` (the book breaks its own promise or its genre's).
- `lens` — the craft file that explains it in depth (`craft/*.md`).
- `detector` — the pre-pass that finds candidates for it (`signals/`).

Everything here is written for this app, in its own words, from concepts
that belong to the craft as a whole.

## A — Market and positioning

### A.hook

notices: whether the premise holds in one sentence a stranger would repeat
asks: What is this book, in one line?
signals: no single situation, want or danger that could lead a jacket copy; a premise that needs its backstory to be interesting
severity: contract

### A.promise

notices: what the opening pages promise the buyer, and whether the rest honours it
asks: What did page one sell me?
signals: an opening in one register or genre and a book in another; a question raised early and never made central
severity: contract

### A.first-page

notices: the first line, first page and first chapter judged as the sample a reader decides on
asks: Would someone who picked this up read page two?
signals: weather, waking up or backstory before anything is at stake; no voice, person or tension on the first page
exceptions: a genre whose readers expect a slow, atmospheric opening, when the atmosphere is itself unsettling
severity: contract
lens: stakes

### A.length

notices: the book's length against the range its shelf expects
asks: Is this the length its readers expect to hold?
signals: a word count far outside the declared genre's usual range
exceptions: the writer's target length is deliberate and stated in the project
severity: craft

### A.category

notices: whether the prose and the story agree on one shelf — literary, upmarket or commercial
asks: Which shelf is this, and does every page agree?
signals: plot-driven commercial structure told in slow, interior literary prose, or the reverse, without the blend being the point
severity: craft

### A.comps

notices: whether the comparable titles the writer names fit the book they wrote
asks: Would a reader of those books feel at home here?
signals: comparables that share a subject but not a tone, pace or readership
exceptions: never cite what a named book contains — its content is not in front of you
severity: craft

### A.series

notices: whether the ending closes this book while opening the next, when a series is planned
asks: Does it close and still open?
signals: a planned series whose first book resolves nothing, or resolves everything
severity: craft

### A.title

notices: whether the title promises the book the reader gets
asks: Does the title promise this book?
signals: a title in another genre's register; a title that names a minor element
severity: craft

## B — Structure

### B.causality

notices: events joined by "and then" where "therefore" or "but" belongs
asks: Why does this follow from that?
signals: a scene that would read the same if the one before it were cut; coincidence that rescues or dooms the protagonist
exceptions: a picaresque or episodic shape the writer declared
severity: contract
lens: scene

### B.inciting

notices: when the event that starts the story proper lands
asks: Where does the story actually start?
signals: chapters of routine before anything disturbs it; the disturbance summarised rather than dramatised
severity: clarity
lens: arc

### B.proportions

notices: the length of each movement of the story and whether the middle turns
asks: Is the middle doing work?
signals: a middle that repeats the same obstacle; no reversal or revelation near the midpoint; an ending crammed into its last pages
exceptions: the declared narrative structure puts its beats elsewhere
severity: contract

### B.scene-turn

notices: scenes that end with nothing changed
asks: What is different when this scene ends?
signals: a scene whose opening and closing situations are the same for everyone in it
severity: contract
lens: scene

### B.scene-goal

notices: a scene or chapter whose viewpoint character wants nothing in it
asks: What does she want in this scene, and what stands in the way?
signals: a viewpoint character who only observes, travels or remembers for a whole scene
exceptions: a deliberate pause after a blow, when the character is processing it
severity: clarity
lens: scene

### B.scene-sequel

notices: the rhythm of action and aftermath — where the reader and the character get to feel what happened
asks: Where does the reader breathe?
signals: blow after blow with no reaction; or long reflection with nothing to reflect on
severity: craft
lens: scene

### B.stakes

notices: stakes that do not rise, and stakes nobody can name
asks: What is lost if she walks away now?
signals: the same danger at the same size chapter after chapter; a threat to something the reader was never shown the character values
severity: contract
lens: stakes

### B.flaw-failure

notices: setbacks that come from chance or other people's convenience rather than from the protagonist's own flaw
asks: Which of her own failings caused this setback?
signals: failures caused by weather, bad luck or an ally's sudden stupidity; a flaw that is named but never costs anything
severity: contract
lens: arc

### B.premature-peak

notices: high drama before the reader has any reason to care about the people in it
asks: Does the reader care yet, or only watch?
signals: a death, battle or catastrophe in the first pages involving characters we have not met
exceptions: a prologue whose purpose is the question it raises, if the question is then carried
severity: clarity
lens: empathy

### B.tension

notices: the tension curve across the book, and the flat stretches in it
asks: Where does it sag?
signals: several consecutive sections with no open question, threat or desire in play
severity: clarity
lens: stakes

### B.chapter-end

notices: chapter endings that give the reader a reason to stop
asks: Why turn the page?
signals: a chapter that ends with a character going to sleep, a question answered and nothing new asked
exceptions: the end of a part or of the book
severity: craft

### B.setup-payoff

notices: setups never paid off, and payoffs never set up
asks: Where was this earned?
signals: a skill, object or fact that saves the day without having been shown; a loaded detail that never returns
severity: contract

### B.subplot

notices: subplots that never touch the main line
asks: What does this thread do to the spine?
signals: a subplot whose resolution changes nothing in the main story; one that disappears for many chapters
severity: craft

### B.reveal

notices: who knows what, and when — the economy of information between characters and reader
asks: What does the reader know that the hero does not?
signals: information withheld only by the narrator's silence; a reveal the reader already had; dramatic irony left unused
severity: clarity

### B.ending

notices: whether the ending keeps the promise the book made
asks: Is this the ending this book owed its reader?
signals: a resolution by coincidence or by a character introduced late; a climax the protagonist watches rather than acts in
severity: contract
lens: moral-decision

## C — Character

### C.want-need

notices: the gap between what a character wants and what they need
asks: What does she want, and what does she actually need?
signals: a protagonist with a goal and no inner lack, or an inner lack that never meets the goal
severity: craft
lens: arc

### C.flaw

notices: a flaw that is stated rather than shown in action, or that the arc never touches
asks: Where do we see the flaw cost her something?
signals: a flaw described by the narrator or another character but never acted on; a flaw that is gone at the end without a scene that changed it
severity: contract
lens: arc

### C.three-lines

notices: the story's external line, internal line and relationship line — one missing, or not touching the others
asks: What is she fighting outside, inside, and with the people closest to her?
signals: a plot with no inner change; an inner change with no relationship it strains; relationships untouched by the plot
severity: craft
lens: arc

### C.agency

notices: a protagonist things happen to
asks: Who chose this?
signals: turning points decided by other characters or by events; a protagonist rescued at the climax
exceptions: a deliberately passive narrator in short fiction, when passivity is the subject
severity: contract
lens: arc

### C.motive

notices: motives the text has not earned
asks: Why would he do that — here, now?
signals: a decision that serves the plot and contradicts what we know of the person; a reversal of loyalty with no scene that causes it
severity: clarity
lens: motivation-reaction

### C.arc

notices: change that is asserted rather than shown
asks: Where did she change, and what made her?
signals: a character who behaves differently at the end with no scene where the change is paid for
severity: contract
lens: arc

### C.consistency

notices: behaviour that contradicts the person already established
asks: Is this still him?
signals: a sudden skill, fear or opinion the character never had; a voice that shifts for no reason
exceptions: a contradiction the text itself notices and uses
severity: clarity

### C.antagonist

notices: an opposition too weak to matter
asks: Why would the other side win?
signals: an antagonist absent for long stretches, easily fooled, or evil without a reason of their own
severity: contract

### C.cast

notices: characters who blur together, and more characters than the story uses
asks: Could these two be one person?
signals: two characters with the same function; names introduced in a cluster; a character the reader must be reminded of
severity: clarity

### C.relationship

notices: relationships that do not move
asks: What is between them now that was not before?
signals: two characters whose scenes together could be reordered without loss
severity: craft

### C.empathy

notices: a protagonist the reader is not invested in — no threat, no undeserved suffering, no desire to follow — confused with one the reader does not like
asks: Why do I want to follow her, even when I do not like her?
signals: long stretches where the protagonist is safe, comfortable and wants nothing; an attempt to make her likeable instead of making the reader care
severity: contract
lens: empathy

### C.rootability

notices: reasons to care about the protagonist's success
asks: Why do I want her to win?
signals: a goal the reader cannot share or understand; a protagonist who is the worst person in their world with nothing that sets them apart
exceptions: a book whose point is a protagonist the reader fears for or judges, when the book knows it
severity: contract
lens: empathy

### C.moral-decision

notices: a climactic choice that costs nothing, or that the character never has to make
asks: What does she have to give up to choose?
signals: a climax won by skill or luck alone; a dilemma with a painless way out
severity: contract
lens: moral-decision

## D — Theme and meaning

### D.controlling-idea

notices: what the book is about, underneath the plot
asks: What is this book arguing?
signals: a story with no recurring question; a theme stated in dialogue and nowhere enacted
severity: craft

### D.premise

notices: scenes that argue a different premise from the one the book is making
asks: What would this scene prove, taken alone — and does the book agree?
signals: a subplot or ending that contradicts the book's own argument without meaning to
severity: craft
lens: premise

### D.motif

notices: motifs and images that recur, or that fizzle
asks: What does the recurring thing mean by the end?
signals: an image repeated without change; a symbol introduced once and dropped
severity: craft

### D.subtext

notices: everything said out loud
asks: What is not being said?
signals: characters explaining their feelings exactly; the narrator stating the theme
severity: craft

### D.resolution

notices: an ending that settles the plot but not the question the book asked
asks: Did the theme end, or only the story?
signals: a final chapter that ties events and leaves the central question untouched
severity: craft
lens: premise

## E — Point of view and narrative distance

### E.head-hop

notices: point-of-view shifts inside a scene
asks: Whose head are we in?
signals: two characters' private thoughts or sensations reported in the same scene without a break
exceptions: an omniscient narrator the writer declared, used consistently
severity: clarity
lens: pov

### E.discipline

notices: a viewpoint choice that seems accidental
asks: Why this viewpoint for this scene?
signals: a scene told by a character with nothing at stake in it while the one who has everything at stake is present
severity: craft
lens: pov

### E.distance

notices: psychic distance that wobbles
asks: How close is the camera?
signals: deep interior thought followed by a remote, summarising sentence and back, with no reason
exceptions: a deliberate pull-back at the end of a scene
severity: craft
lens: pov

### E.filter

notices: perception and thought verbs standing between the reader and what the viewpoint character perceives
asks: Can the reader see it directly, instead of watching her see it?
signals: "she saw", "he heard", "she felt", "he noticed", "she realised" framing a sensation or thought the scene could simply render
exceptions: a distant or retrospective narrator in the writer's declared style; the act of perceiving is itself the event; the verb marks real uncertainty
severity: craft
lens: pov
detector: perception-verbs

### E.tense

notices: tense slips
asks: When is this narrated from?
signals: present-tense verbs in a past-tense narration outside dialogue and general truths
exceptions: a declared present-tense frame, or a frame story
severity: clarity

### E.reliability

notices: a narrator whose reliability is unclear by accident
asks: Should I trust this voice?
signals: a narrator who is sometimes wrong in ways the book never acknowledges
severity: craft
lens: pov

### E.knowledge-leak

notices: a viewpoint knowing what it cannot
asks: How does she know that?
signals: the viewpoint character naming another's private thought, or a fact they were never told
severity: clarity
lens: pov

### E.stimulus-response

notices: a reaction that arrives before its cause, or a conclusion that outruns what the reader has been shown
asks: What did she perceive first, and what did she do because of it?
signals: "she flinched as the door slammed" order inversions; a character understanding a clue before the reader has seen it
severity: craft
lens: motivation-reaction

### E.commentary

notices: the narrator judging, explaining or addressing the reader from outside the viewpoint the scene has chosen
asks: Who is speaking here — the character, or someone standing outside the story?
signals: moral asides; explanations of what a scene means; "little did she know"; addresses to the reader
exceptions: a commenting narrator the writer declared as the book's voice
severity: craft
lens: pov

### E.pov-diction

notices: words, knowledge or references the viewpoint character would not have — including anachronism inside a period viewpoint
asks: Would she think it in these words?
signals: technical or modern vocabulary in the thoughts of a character who would not know it; units, dates or names from outside the character's world
severity: clarity
lens: pov

## F — Prose

### F.rhythm

notices: monotone sentence length and shape
asks: Read it aloud — where does it drag?
signals: runs of sentences of the same length and construction; paragraphs that all open the same way
exceptions: a monotony that enacts the scene (exhaustion, repetition, ritual)
severity: craft
detector: sentence-rhythm

### F.echo

notices: echoed words and structures at close range
asks: Did you mean to say it twice?
signals: the same content word twice within a few lines; the same sentence frame repeated
exceptions: deliberate repetition for rhythm or emphasis
severity: craft
detector: word-echo

### F.cliche

notices: clichés and dead metaphors
asks: Whose phrase is this?
signals: stock similes; stock bodily reactions (a breath she didn't know she was holding)
exceptions: a cliché in a character's mouth, as characterisation
severity: craft

### F.show-tell

notices: emotion named instead of rendered
asks: Can I feel it instead of being told?
signals: "she was angry", "he felt sad" where a gesture, choice or line of dialogue could carry it
exceptions: summary that moves the reader briskly across time on purpose
severity: craft

### F.economy

notices: the ten percent that can go
asks: What survives the cut?
signals: sentences that repeat what the previous one showed; stage directions for every movement
severity: craft

### F.purple

notices: ornament that blocks the picture
asks: What is the plain version, and is it better?
signals: strings of adjectives and metaphors around one object; abstraction piled on abstraction
exceptions: a lush register that is the book's declared voice, kept consistently
severity: craft

### F.diction

notices: the almost-right word
asks: Is this the word, or its cousin?
signals: near-synonyms used loosely; a register that slips for one word
severity: craft

### F.metaphor

notices: mixed or collapsing images
asks: Does the image hold to the end of the sentence?
signals: two metaphors fused into one image that cannot exist
severity: craft

### F.voice-drift

notices: passages that drift away from the writer's established voice
asks: Is this you, or you on a tired day?
signals: a stretch whose sentence length, vocabulary or register differs from the rest of the book without a reason in the story
severity: craft

### F.hedge

notices: qualifiers that blur a picture the scene should commit to
asks: Is it almost, or is it?
signals: "almost", "rather", "somewhat", "seemed", "a little" softening concrete description
exceptions: the viewpoint character is genuinely unsure; dialogue
severity: craft
detector: hedges

### F.attempt

notices: "tried to", "began to", "started to" hiding whether the thing happened
asks: Did it happen?
signals: an attempt verb where the action simply occurs
exceptions: the attempt fails, and the failure matters
severity: craft
detector: attempt-verbs

### F.manner-adverb

notices: manner adverbs doing the work a stronger verb should do
asks: Is there a verb that already means this?
signals: "walked slowly", "said angrily", "closed the door quietly"
exceptions: dialogue; an adverb that changes the meaning rather than intensifying it
severity: craft
detector: manner-adverbs

### F.adjective-stack

notices: stacked or evaluative adjectives where one concrete one would carry the picture
asks: Which one of these does the reader see?
signals: two or more adjectives on one noun; judging adjectives (beautiful, terrible, amazing) in narration
severity: craft

## G — Dialogue

### G.exposition

notices: characters telling each other what both already know, for the reader's sake
asks: Would they say this to each other?
signals: "as you know"; a character reciting shared history to someone who lived it
severity: clarity
lens: dialogue

### G.tags

notices: tag clutter and tags that tell the reader how to hear the line
asks: Does the line need the tag?
signals: tags on every line of a two-person exchange; tags other than the plain ones doing emotional work
exceptions: a tag that carries sound the line cannot (a whisper, a shout)
severity: craft
lens: dialogue
detector: dialogue-tags

### G.beats

notices: action beats that stage nothing
asks: What is the hand doing, and why?
signals: nods, sighs and shrugs on every line; beats that repeat the line's meaning
severity: craft
lens: dialogue

### G.subtext

notices: characters saying exactly what they mean
asks: What are they really fighting about?
signals: arguments conducted in explicit statements of feeling; no line that means more than it says
severity: craft
lens: dialogue

### G.voices

notices: characters who sound alike
asks: Whose line is this, with the name covered?
signals: the same vocabulary, sentence length and humour in every mouth
severity: clarity
lens: dialogue

### G.dialect

notices: dialect rendered as caricature
asks: Is this a voice or a costume?
signals: heavy phonetic spelling; dialect given only to comic or lower-status characters
severity: craft
lens: authenticity

### G.talking-heads

notices: long runs of dialogue with no body, place or action to anchor them
asks: Where are they, and what are they doing while they talk?
signals: many consecutive lines of speech with no narration between them
exceptions: a rapid exchange whose speed is the point, kept short
severity: clarity
lens: dialogue
detector: dialogue-runs

## H — Worldbuilding and exposition

### H.info-dump

notices: exposition delivered in blocks
asks: Does the reader need this now?
signals: paragraphs of history, geography or system explanation with no character acting in them
severity: clarity
lens: exposition

### H.rules

notices: magic, technology or social rules broken without cost
asks: What does this cost, by the book's own rules?
signals: a power that grows when the plot needs it; a rule stated early and ignored later
severity: contract

### H.white-room

notices: scenes with no place in them
asks: Where are we standing?
signals: dialogue or action with no setting detail for a whole scene
severity: clarity

### H.timing

notices: exposition that arrives before the reader has asked for it
asks: Has the reader asked yet?
signals: explanation of a system before any scene needs it; backstory before the present has any pull
severity: craft
lens: exposition

### H.senses

notices: a world that is only seen
asks: What does it sound, smell and feel like?
signals: settings rendered by sight alone
severity: craft

### H.terms

notices: invented terms used inconsistently
asks: Is this the same thing as before?
signals: one invented thing with two names, or one name for two things; capitalisation that changes
severity: clarity

### H.jargon

notices: technical terms, foreign words or units the reader cannot picture, used for authenticity's sake
asks: Can the reader see what this word names?
signals: unexplained specialist vocabulary; precise measures the character would not think in
exceptions: jargon that characterises a speaker who would use it, where context carries the meaning
severity: clarity
lens: exposition
detector: numbers-in-narration

## I — Continuity and fact

### I.names

notices: names and spellings that drift
asks: Which spelling is this character's?
signals: one character spelled two ways; a nickname used before it is introduced
severity: clarity

### I.physical

notices: descriptions that change
asks: Weren't her eyes grey?
signals: eye, hair or height details that differ between sections
severity: clarity

### I.timeline

notices: dates, ages and durations that do not add up
asks: How long has it been?
signals: a pregnancy, journey or season that lasts the wrong length; an age that does not match a date
severity: clarity

### I.geography

notices: distances and places that move
asks: Can she get there by noon?
signals: travel times that change; a building on two sides of the same street
severity: clarity

### I.objects

notices: objects that appear, vanish or change hands without a scene
asks: Who has the key now?
signals: an item used by a character who gave it away; a lost object present again
severity: clarity

### I.knowledge

notices: characters knowing what they were never told
asks: When did he learn that?
signals: a character acting on information from a scene they were not in
severity: clarity

### I.real-world

notices: real-world facts and period detail
asks: Was that true in that place, at that time?
signals: technology, laws, prices, speech or objects from the wrong period; real places described wrongly
severity: clarity

### I.style-sheet

notices: the book's own conventions — capitals, numbers, hyphenation, spellings — applied unevenly
asks: Which way does this book spell it?
signals: the same word capitalised and not; numbers as digits and as words in the same context
severity: craft

## J — Mechanics

### J.grammar

notices: grammar that is wrong rather than stylistic
asks: Is this an error, or a choice the prose style declares?
signals: agreement, tense-sequence and pronoun errors in narration
exceptions: a declared departure from convention; a character's speech
severity: craft

### J.punctuation

notices: punctuation errors that change or blur the meaning
asks: Does the punctuation say what you mean?
signals: comma splices that read as run-ons; missing closing marks
severity: craft

### J.dialogue-marks

notices: dialogue punctuation that departs from the book's own convention
asks: Which marks does this book use for speech?
signals: guillemets, dashes and quotation marks mixed in one book
exceptions: the writer's prose style declares a mixed convention with a rule
severity: craft
detector: dialogue-marks

### J.homophone

notices: homophones and near-misses a spellchecker cannot see
asks: Is this the word you meant?
signals: a real word in place of the intended one
severity: craft

### J.house-style

notices: departures from the conventions the writer's prose style declares
asks: Does this follow the book's own rule?
signals: anything that contradicts a stated convention on numbers, spellings, italics or punctuation
severity: craft

## K — Authenticity and representation

### K.stereotype

notices: characters built from a stereotype
asks: Is this a person, or a type?
signals: a character whose traits are the familiar shorthand for a group, with nothing individual beside them
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.harmful-trope

notices: tropes that repeat a harmful story about a group
asks: Which old story does this repeat?
signals: the only queer character dying to move the plot; a minority character who exists to help the white lead; disability as something to cure, punish or use as a metaphor; a partner killed to motivate the hero
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.agency

notices: marginalised characters with less agency or interiority than the rest of the cast
asks: Who gets a point of view, and who is scenery?
signals: characters from a marginalised group who never want, choose or think on the page
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.token

notices: one character standing for a whole group
asks: Is she allowed to be one person?
signals: a single character whose every line is about their identity; the group judged by what one member does
severity: craft
lens: authenticity

### K.othering

notices: description that marks some people as other
asks: Who is being described as different, and from whose point of view?
signals: food words for skin; "exotic"; describing ethnicity only for characters who are not white
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.terminology

notices: terms for people and groups that have dated or that wound
asks: Is this the word the community uses for itself today?
signals: outdated or slurring terms in narration, outside the voice of a character who would use them
exceptions: a period or character voice the book frames as such
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.trauma

notices: trauma used as decoration or as a plot device
asks: Whose pain is this, and what is it for?
signals: sexual violence as backstory shorthand; suffering that happens to one character to change another
severity: contract
lens: authenticity

### K.expert

notices: expert detail — medicine, law, police procedure, weapons, sailing, period life — that a professional would wince at
asks: Would someone who does this for a living believe it?
signals: procedures done wrongly or impossibly fast; equipment used in ways it cannot be
severity: clarity
lens: authenticity

## L — Genre

### L.promise

notices: whether the book keeps the contract of its declared genre — its obligatory scenes, its conventions and the ending its readers came for
asks: Would a reader of this genre feel the book kept its promise?
signals: an obligatory scene missing or happening off the page; a convention broken without the break being the point
exceptions: a deliberate subversion the book sets up and pays off
severity: contract

### M.attachment
