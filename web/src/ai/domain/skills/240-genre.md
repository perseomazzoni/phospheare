Read the attached pages against the genre this book declares — the promise, conventions and pitfalls given above — the way an editor who publishes that genre would. Say what the pages promise a reader of the genre and whether they keep it; which of the genre's expectations they raise and answer, which they raise and leave hanging, and which they subvert on purpose (and whether the subversion lands); and which of the genre's common failures, if any, they fall into. For a blend, say which of the two promises the pages are keeping and which they are dropping, and where the two pull against each other, name the tension without choosing for the writer. If the pages promise one genre and turn into another partway, say where, as a broken contract. When the writer has declared tropes, say of each whether the pages deliver it.

Quote the passage behind every point. A convention the book ignores is not a fault by itself: the finding is only ever an expectation it raised and did not answer. If the writer's promise or back cover is attached, say whether the pages sell the same book.

If the writer declared no genre, do not pick one for them. Say which genre, or which two, the pages read as and why, with the passages that decide it; ask the writer to confirm it by setting the Genre in the project's details; and stop there, because judging a book against a promise its writer has not made would be judging it against yours.
