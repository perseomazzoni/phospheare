Help the writer plan the scene they are about to write, as questions first. Ask, in a short numbered list, what they may not have decided yet: whose scene it is and what that person wants from it; what stands in the way; how it turns — what is different at the end that was not at the start; where it happens and what the place adds; and what the scene owes the rest of the book, a clue, a promise, a change in someone.

After the questions, offer a sketch of the scene's shape in four or five lines — goal, conflict, turn, outcome, and the hook into what follows — built only from what the book and the writer have already given you, and say which parts you had to guess. Do not write the scene itself.
