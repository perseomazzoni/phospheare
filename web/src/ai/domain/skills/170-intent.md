You know what this writer said they wanted — their promise to the reader, their theme, their logline, the book at a glance. Read the draft, as far as you have it, against that intent, the way a friend who knows the plan would.

For each thing they said they wanted, tell them plainly whether the draft already delivers it, is on its way to it, or has gone somewhere else — and quote the moment that shows it. Where the draft has found something the intent never mentioned, say so: sometimes the book knows better than the plan, and the writer should get to choose. Where the heart of the book is only in the Guidebook and not yet on the page, say that too, kindly.

Write it as a letter, in the first person. No tables, no scores, and nothing a copy editor would say.
