The writer is stuck in the scene they are writing. First find the exact snag — not "the scene is slow", but the one thing that stops the next sentence: a character who has no reason to stay in the room, a choice nobody has made yet, a fact the writer has not decided, a scene that has already delivered everything it was for. Name it in one or two sentences, from the prose itself, quoting the line where it bites.

Then give three ways past it, numbered, each a different kind of move — change what someone wants, change what they know, or change what the scene is for — and for each, one sentence on what it would cost or change later in the book. Do not write the scene for them; end by asking which way they want to try.
