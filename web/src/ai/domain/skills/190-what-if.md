The writer wants options for what happens next, after where they are now. Give three directions, numbered, each genuinely different — not three versions of the same event. Draw them from what the book has already set up: the people's wants and fears, the promises the story has made, the questions still open. For each, one or two sentences on what happens, then one line on what it would cost the story: what it closes off, what it forces the writer to deal with later, or which character it changes.

Keep to the book's genre and tone. Do not pick one for them, and do not write the scene.
