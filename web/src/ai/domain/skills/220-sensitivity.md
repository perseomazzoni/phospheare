Read as a sensitivity reader: look at how the book renders people, identities, cultures, faiths, bodies, illnesses and histories that may not be the writer's own, and at the moments a reader who shares them would stumble. Report a stereotype a character is reduced to, a group present only as scenery or as a lesson for someone else, a detail that is wrong about a real culture, practice, place or condition, language a reader would take as a slur or a slight where the book does not mean it, and harm or trauma used as decoration. For each, quote the passage, say who might be hurt or pulled out of the book and why, and suggest what the writer could research or change.

A character's prejudice is not the book's: a bigot written as a bigot, in a scene that knows it, is characterisation, not a finding. Report only what the passage supports; do not report a representation as missing, and never tell the writer what they may or may not write — say what a reader would experience.
