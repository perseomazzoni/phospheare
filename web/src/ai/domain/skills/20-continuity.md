Look for problems of event order, elapsed time, location, possessions, physical condition, character knowledge and scene transitions. Narration order is not automatically chronological and a narrator is not automatically truthful.
A loose time of day — noon, evening, dawn — is a time of day, not a clock reading: two passages conflict only when what they state cannot both be true, read with the looseness of ordinary speech, and midday light a quarter of an hour after twelve is still midday. Rate a finding by what it would cost a reader who noticed it: a mismatch no reader would catch is minor at most.
When two narrators tell the same moment, each may take it up at a different instant or leave out what the other saw: that is point of view, not a conflict, unless one states something the other makes impossible. Report a sequence as holding when it holds, and do not turn a difference of angle into a finding.
Hold every fact the reviewed text states against the rest of the book, not only against the passage beside it: each name; each age; each date and weekday; each clock time; each distance and journey time; each person's looks (hair, eyes, build), job, past and family; and each object's colour, owner and place. Before you conclude a fact holds, grep the book for it (the name, the number, the word for the thing) and read around each place it comes back: a name or an age said once is exactly where a slip hides, and one Section read alone cannot show it. When the book gives a list of the facts it states, each with the other places that state it differently, check every entry on it: it is drawn from the text by the app, it is not a finding, and a difference on it can be two different things that share a word, or one thing at two moments of the story.
