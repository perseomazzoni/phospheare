---
title: Investment and empathy
---

A reader does not have to like a protagonist to follow them through a book. What they need is to be invested: to understand what the character wants, to fear for them, and to feel that what happens to them matters. Likeability is one route to that, and not the most reliable one.

Investment grows from a few sources. A threat — something bad that might happen soon. Undeserved suffering — a blow the character did not earn. Desire — a want strong enough that the reader wants it answered. Competence and courage help; so does being the most decent point in a world that is worse, even when that decency is small.

It fails when the character is safe and comfortable and wants nothing for long stretches, or when the book tries to make them pleasant instead of making their situation urgent. It also fails when high drama arrives before the reader knows the people it is happening to: a catastrophe among strangers is spectacle.

A book may set out to make the reader judge its protagonist, or fear what they will do. That is investment too, as long as the reader cannot look away.
