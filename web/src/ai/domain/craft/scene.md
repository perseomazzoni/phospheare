---
title: The scene
---

A scene is a unit of change. Someone wants something in it, something stands in the way, and by its end the situation has turned — better or worse, but different. A scene that ends where it began is description, however well written.

The engine is a viewpoint character with a goal for this scene, even a small one: to get out of the room, to hide what they know, to be forgiven. Conflict is whatever opposes that goal; the outcome is usually a setback, or a success that costs more than it should, because an easy win closes the question the reader was holding.

After a strong scene comes its aftermath — the character absorbs what happened, weighs the options and decides what to do next. That reflective beat is where the reader feels the blow land and where the next goal is born. Books that skip it read as breathless; books that dwell in it read as stalled.

Events inside a scene should cause one another. When a scene joins its events only by "and then", nothing causes anything, and the reader stops expecting consequences.
