---
title: Exposition
---

Every story has things the reader must know: the history, the rules of the world, what a word means. Exposition fails when it arrives as a block the story stops for, and when it arrives before the reader has any reason to want it.

The reader's curiosity is the best delivery system. Information given in answer to a question the reader is already asking is absorbed with pleasure; the same information given unasked is homework. So the craft is mostly timing — hold the explanation until a scene needs it, and give only as much as that scene needs.

Exposition carried by a character in conflict reads as story: a detail revealed because someone uses it, fights about it, or is surprised by it. Exposition carried by the narrator in a pause reads as a lecture, however elegant.

Specialist vocabulary is its own case. Precise terms can make a world feel real, but only if the reader can picture what they name, and only in the mouth or mind of someone who would use them. A measure or term the viewpoint character would not think in is the author showing their research.
