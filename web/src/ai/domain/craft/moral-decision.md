---
title: The decisive choice
---

The climax of a character-driven story is usually a choice, not a fight. The protagonist faces two options, each with a real cost, and what they choose shows who they have become. A climax won by skill, strength or luck alone shows only that they were strong enough.

A true dilemma has no painless exit. If one option is plainly right and costs nothing, there is no decision, only a delay. The choice is sharpest when both sides are things the character values — and when the reader, too, is not sure which they would pick.

The choice should be set up long before it arrives: the values in conflict are the ones the book has been testing all along, and the flaw the character carried is exactly what makes the right choice hard. That is how the climax answers the book's argument instead of merely ending its plot.

It fails when the choice is made for the character by events, when the cost is waived at the last moment, or when the book never asked the question its ending answers.
