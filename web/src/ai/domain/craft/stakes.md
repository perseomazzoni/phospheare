---
title: Stakes
---

Stakes are what the character stands to lose. The clearest way to find them is to ask what happens if the character simply walks away — if nothing does, there are no stakes, only activity.

A reader invests in proportion to what is at risk and to how well they know it matters. That second half is where stakes most often fail: a threat to a sister we have never seen the protagonist love is a threat on paper only. The loss has to be made concrete before it is threatened.

Stakes should tighten as the story goes on — more at risk, less room to retreat, costs that cannot be undone — and they should stay about the same thing, so the reader's fear has somewhere to accumulate. A book that swaps its stakes every few chapters resets the reader's investment each time.

Tension is stakes held open over time: a question the reader wants answered, a danger not yet arrived. It sags when every question closes as soon as it opens, or when a stretch of the book has nothing in play at all.
