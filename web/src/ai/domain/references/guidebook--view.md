The writer's map of knowledge: what is true, what each character knows or believes, what the reader knows, when things are discovered, and what stays unexplained.

What is true is the writer's established fact. What a character believes may be wrong on purpose: a character acting on a false belief is no slip. The discoveries set when knowledge arrives: weigh a scene where someone seems to know something before its discovery, and say so when it matters. What remains unexplained is undecided or withheld on purpose: never treat it as a gap to fill.
