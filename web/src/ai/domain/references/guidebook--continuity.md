The writer's continuity notebook: where the book's names, dates, ages and relationships are kept, which questions are still open, and which contradictions the writer already knows about.

Much of it points to other documents; a pointer is not a fact. What it states outright is the writer's established fact. The open questions are undecided on purpose: never treat one as settled, and never report the manuscript for not settling it. The contradictions to resolve are already on the writer's list: when the pages still show one, say it is the known one, and never call it fixed unless the pages show the fix. A line that says how the book must be read (two lives told in turn, a narrator who is wrong) tells you how to judge a disagreement: apply it to the places in question before you rely on it.
