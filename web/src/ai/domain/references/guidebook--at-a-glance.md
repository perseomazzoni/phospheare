The writer's own summary of the book: its genre, its readers, its setting, its tone, its point of view and tense, and the sensitive content it carries.

Treat it as the frame every answer works inside: the book's genre and readers set what a good answer sounds like. Point of view and tense are conventions the prose keeps: a slip from them is a finding, unless the prose-style page lists it as a deliberate departure. The sensitive-content field says what the writer knows the book contains and how they want it handled; it scopes a sensitivity reading, it is not a list of problems.
